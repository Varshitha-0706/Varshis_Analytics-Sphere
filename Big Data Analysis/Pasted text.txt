Introduction
The bivariate normal distribution models two continuous random variables (X, Y ) that follow a joint Gaussian law. It
generalizes the univariate normal distribution to two dimensions, capturing both individual variation and linear dependence
between variables.

NPTEL

Real-Life Motivation
Height and weight of individuals.
Length and width of leaves.
Returns on two correlated stocks.
Exam scores in two related subjects.
The bivariate normal captures both individual normality and the correlation structure between the two variables.

Special Case: Independence
If ρ = 0, then

f (x, y) = fX (x)fY (y),

and X and Y are independent. The joint density contours are circular (if σX = σY ) or axis-aligned ellipses otherwise.

NPTEL

Geometry of the Distribution
The joint density of (X, Y ) forms elliptical contours:
ρ > 0: Ellipses tilt upward (positive correlation).
ρ < 0: Ellipses tilt downward (negative correlation).
ρ = 0: Circles or axis-aligned ellipses (independence).

Important Properties
Marginals and conditionals are normal.
E[X] = μX , E[Y ] = μY .
Cov(X, Y ) = ρσX σY .
E[X|Y ] and E[Y |X] are linear.
The bivariate normal is the only joint distribution where linear correlation fully describes dependence.
